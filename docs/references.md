# References and provenance

These references are transcribed from the original project notes and local
research collection. Downloaded third-party PDFs and third-party
figures are not redistributed here.

## Research sources

- Andrew Fowler, Andrew Young, and Matthew Hudelson, *Minesweeper: A Statistical
  and Computational Analysis* (2004). The notes use this work for compatible
  configurations and the perimeter probability discussion.
- *A Mathematical Introduction to the Game of Minesweeper* (1997), retained in
  the original literature collection.
- *Automated Strategies for the Game Minesweeper* (2006), retained in the original
  literature collection.
- *Minesweeper: Where to Probe?* (2012), retained in the original literature collection.
- Gareth Cook, *Minesweeper could hold key to Net security* (2000), cited in the
  original annotated bibliography.
- [Minesweeper first-click strategy](https://minesweepergame.com/strategy/first-click.php).
- [Minesweeper strategy wiki](https://www.minesweeper.info/wiki/Strategy).

The incomplete bibliographic entries above retain the information available in
the supplied files rather than guessing authors, publication venues, or dates.

## Code provenance

The original `références.odt` identifies the
[AskPython Minesweeper tutorial](https://www.askpython.com/python/examples/create-minesweeper-using-python)
as the source of the initial game code. Console display, mine placement, clue
calculation, and recursive reveal routines in the original project follow that
starting point. This attribution is retained for the derived game routines.

The strategy experiments and research were developed for Dimitri Abitbol's MP*
TIPE. This English edition reorganizes the Python experiments and
documents their behavior and limitations.
